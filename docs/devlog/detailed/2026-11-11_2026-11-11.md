# Devlog — 2026-11-11

One long session took the repository from an empty checkout to a playable game. The shape of the day: contracts first (`src/sim/api.ts`, the block table, the clock), then three headless simulations built by delegated workers in their own worktrees while the session built what the player sees, then integration. The failures that recurred were of two kinds, stated once here. First, things that pass every test and are wrong on screen: all but one of the visual defects below were found by looking at full-resolution captures, none by a gate. Second, measurements that depend on the machine rather than the code: three timing checks failed or passed for reasons that had nothing to do with what they measured.

## Anisotropic filtering forces linear magnification on Direct3D

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11, first render of the island

**Action:** Block textures are sampled through `tileSample` (`src/render/shaders/common.ts`), which snaps the lookup to texel centres with a one-pixel antialiased edge, on a texture left at linear magnification.

**Result:** 16-pixel tiles are crisp squares up close and still filter and mip at distance.

**Reasoning:** Nearest magnification was the obvious setting and it was ignored: with anisotropy on, which the ground needs at grazing angles, ANGLE on Direct3D magnifies linearly whatever the filter says. Turning anisotropy off traded blurry texels for a shimmering ground.

**Validation:** Looked at in captures at several distances. No gate.

## Ambient occlusion was reading the painted relief

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11, while inspecting light shafts

**Action:** `SSAO_FS` rebuilds the face normal from four depth taps instead of reading the G-buffer normal.

**Result:** On a flat birch wall, mean brightness with occlusion on was 154.1 against 173.7 off (11.3 % darker); it is now 174.0 against 173.9.

**Reasoning:** The G-buffer normal is tilted texel by texel by each tile's relief, and a tilted normal sees the flat face it sits on as an occluder. The symptom looked like blurred textures, so bloom and antialiasing were suspected first; captures with each effect off in turn showed the halo followed occlusion alone.

**Validation:** `ambient occlusion leaves a flat, high-contrast wall alone` in the browser gate, proved red against the old shader. Its first version passed against the defect: every `step` in one `page.evaluate` runs without yielding, the mesh workers' answers never arrived, and it was comparing sky with sky. It now waits for the mesh pool and fails if any probe is sky-blue.

## Sunset was an orange world with black shadows

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11, first capture of wildlife (taken at 18:02 by accident)

**Action:** A twilight term (`twilight()` in `src/render/atmosphere.ts`, mirrored in GLSL) adds a blue dome and a warm band toward the sun to the sky lookup and the matching fill to the ambient light. The key light is dimmed further on the horizon and kept from going pure red.

**Result:** With the sun at 1.7 degrees the ambient light was (0.066, 0.049, 0.029) against a key of (2.12, 0.51, 0.03), a ratio of thirty to one, and exposure was 7.3. It is now (0.23, 0.24, 0.34) against (0.86, 0.36, 0.14), exposure 3.0. Twenty-five minutes after sunset the ambient was (0.001, 0.002, 0.003) with exposure 280; it is now (0.073, 0.087, 0.138) with exposure 5.

**Reasoning:** The atmosphere is single scattering, which gives out as soon as the sun is low; the real sky stays lit by light scattered more than once. A multiple-scattering lookup was the principled fix and was not taken: a designed dome shared by the sky and the ambient light costs nothing per pixel and can be tuned by eye. The earlier sunset captures had looked fine only because they were taken twenty minutes before the collapse.

**Validation:** Captures at 17:03, 17:47, 18:07 and 18:32. The browser gate's night test still passes (noon 117.6, midnight 36.0). No gate reads dusk.

## The twilight term was covering for a bug (correction to the section above)

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11, after the first review round

**Action:** `hitsGround` in `src/render/atmosphere.ts` (CPU and GLSL) replaces three tests of the form `raySphere(...).x > 0`. The twilight term described above is removed.

**Result:** Sky ambient with the sun at 1.7 degrees is (0.43, 0.45, 0.53) from the model alone, against (0.066, 0.049, 0.029) with the bug and (0.23, 0.24, 0.34) with the bug plus the designed term. With the sun 0.8 degrees under it is (0.22, 0.18, 0.21); four degrees under, (0.02, 0.015, 0.021). The dome's brightness now falls smoothly: 3.7e-3 with the sun on the horizon, 5.6e-4 at three degrees under, 2.1e-5 at six.

**Reasoning:** `raySphere` returns (big, -big) for a ray that misses the sphere, and "first root positive" read that as a hit. From the ground only a sun within half a degree of level is affected, which is why noon and the earlier sunset captures looked right; from a sample ten kilometres up, any sun within three degrees of level misses the planet, so most of the atmosphere was treated as shadowed exactly when it matters. The section above reasoned from the symptom ("single scattering gives out when the sun is low") to a designed fix without checking the model's numbers against what single scattering should give. An independent reviewer reading `atmosphere.ts` found it in one pass.

**Validation:** `src/render/atmosphere.test.ts`: the sun's light falls monotonically to the horizon with no gap; the dome fades through twenty degrees of sun with no step larger than half per half degree above minus six; the zenith is still lit with the sun four degrees under. Three of its five tests fail against the old test. Captures at 17:47, 18:07, 18:22 and 18:43.

**Code reviewer comments:** Found by review (Codex, lens B, finding F9), not by the author and not by any gate.

## Spilt water drew as solid white

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11, first capture of physics in the game

**Action:** The mesher marks finite water (bit 27 of the second vertex word) and the water shader gives it no surf; surf is kept for the sea and for running rivers.

**Result:** A spill reads as a thin film with sun glints instead of a white sheet.

**Reasoning:** Foam was drawn wherever the bed lay close under the surface, which is the right test for a beach and true everywhere in a puddle. The first fix (surf only near sea level) left the spill white, because finite water also carries flow bits and took the river's branch.

**Validation:** Captures before and after. No gate.

## Three workers, three merges

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11

**Action:** Physics, wildlife and the village were each built headless in an isolated worktree against `src/sim/api.ts` and merged in that order. Each was judged by running its tests on the merged tree, not by its report: 50, 59 and 53 tests.

**Result:** No merge conflicted, because each worker owned one directory. Two contract changes were asked for and made (`LivestockApi.findLivestock` takes kinds; `EntityView.hidden`). The village worker was cut off by a usage limit in the middle of landing and finished when resumed.

## Two simulations that each passed their soak did not work together

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11

**Action:** Wrote `src/life.test.ts`: the village and the wildlife on the island the game generates, light repair attached, two days. Then repaired what it showed.

**Result:** First run: 6 route failures, the hunter carried home by the stuck-walker fallback, nothing hunted in a day and a half, and one tick of 121 ms each night. After: no failures, 3 or 4 kills in 54 hours, trees felled, slowest tick 16 to 26 ms.

**Reasoning:** Four separate causes, none visible on a fixture. (1) Generation chose the densest wood on the island, 180 blocks from the square, and the path search's allowance of 14,000 expansions had been sized on a 128-block test village; the allowance now grows with distance and generation charges a wood for the walk. (2) No game ever stood on the plan's hunting ground, and the animals run from a person at 7 to 16 blocks while the hunt needed 3.5: the hunt had only ever been run against a stub. The hunter now tracks the nearest game and shoots from about 18 blocks; `WildlifeApi.shoot` leaves the body for him to walk to, so meat does not appear in a pack 18 blocks away. (3) Saplings come of age by the day, so every one due became a tree in the same tick. (4) A walker's height changed half-way across a step's edge, so legs sank half a block into every step of a hill; no fixture has a hillside.

**Validation:** The test, red on its first run. The allowance has its own unit test, seen red with the scaling off. Looked at in captures: the hunter aiming, and at the body. What it would miss: anything that takes longer than two days to go wrong.

**Notes:** Still open: on the generated island the wolves' den is high on the mountain, they come down to hunt and cannot find the way back (animals steer locally, with no path search), and they starve within a year. Arrivals from off the map replace them.

## The river read its own bed as the ground

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11

**Action:** World generation's worker rewrote how the river's surface is set (`terrain.ts`).

**Result:** Before, on every seed: the river fell from the lake's level (66) to the sea's (39) within eight points of its start and ran the rest of its course in a gorge about 27 deep, through a slot of open air cut into the lake. After: it leaves the lake through the rim at lake level and comes down in single-block steps.

**Reasoning:** Each point of the course took its level from the terrain at that point, and the terrain there had just been lowered by the point before. The course is now laid first and the surface set from a copy of the ground as it was before anything was cut. The reviewer reported the slot in the lake; the gorge was the same bug and had been taken for a design.

**Validation:** Island tests on three sizes that no water stands beside open air or rests on nothing; before and after captures. The fluid simulation's own test on a generated island counts 92 places where a source stands over lower water and none beside a drop onto land.

**Notes:** Where a lake stands well above the ground outside its rim the river drops that height in one ledge, not a cascade.

## A normal from depth is only good near the camera

**Timestamp:** 2026-11-11

**Action:** The lighting pass needed the face a pixel lies on (for the shadow offset, puddles, snow, caustics), not the painted relief in the G-buffer. The renderer's worker first rebuilt it from depth, as ambient occlusion does, then replaced that with a face code written by the geometry pass.

**Result:** From depth, far cliffs came out white with snow: beyond a few hundred metres one step of the depth buffer is a fair part of the gap between neighbouring pixels, so the rebuilt normal is noise. With the code there are no specks at any distance, and the stripes of wet and dry on walls in rain are gone.

**Reasoning:** The invariant in AGENTS.md said to rebuild from depth; it now says either source, and that depth is only good near the camera. Ambient occlusion works at short range and keeps the depth version.

**Validation:** Captures at 0c98827 and after, near and far, in snow and rain. No unit test: it is a shader.
